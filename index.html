Mise en Vie

A four-day celebration of your life, while you are here to receive it.

Most people receive their finest hour after they are gone. We moved it forward.

The Idea Read The Salt Line
The Idea

You, as the main character.

Mise en place is the chef's discipline: every ingredient found, tested, prepped, and set in its place before the first flame. Mise en Vie applies that discipline to a life. Over four composed days, one person moves through an immersive celebration built entirely from the particulars of their own story, surrounded by the people who supplied them. The rooms of their life, rebuilt and walkable. The dishes nobody has made right in decades, made right. The things usually saved for the eulogy, said out loud, to their face.

It is not a party and it is not a retrospective. It is a work of stewardship, composed to one person, performed once.

The Practice

How a life becomes four days.

Movement I

The Quiet Research

It begins with conversation, not a questionnaire. Our curators sit with you, and then, discreetly and with your blessing, with the people who know you from every angle: family, oldest friends, the colleague, the neighbor, the bartender. Nobody is asked for a tribute. They are asked for particulars. The song that was always on. The exact color of the kitchen on Sunday mornings. The phrase you say without knowing you say it.

Movement II

The Test Kitchenthe real secret

Nothing reaches your four days untested. The recipe is cooked, tasted against living memory, and cooked again until the person who remembers it goes quiet. The record is found on the right format and played on the right machine. The scent is blended and re-blended. Every ingredient that embodies you is rehearsed until it is not a reference to your life but a piece of it.

Movement III

The Archive

Everything gathered in the research and everything captured across the four days is composed into a private family archive: the recordings, the transcripts, the recipes as tested, the score of the days. Held under vaulted, access-controlled retention for exactly as long as your family wishes, and not a day longer. Discretion is not a courtesy here. It is the craft.

The Arc

The Four Days

Day One

The Threshold

Arrival. The world quiets. You are received, softened, held. Nothing is asked of you.

Day Two

The Rooms

The immersive day. Places, eras, and textures of your life, rebuilt and walkable. You move through your own story as its main character.

Day Three

The Long Table

The voices. Your people gathered, the tested dishes served, and the things usually saved for later said now, over food that tastes exactly right.

Day Four

The Fourth Morning

Quiet. Breath. The archive placed in your hands. You leave with the high-water mark on you.
